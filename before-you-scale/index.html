Before you scale

The cost of supervision mismatch in PRM distillation

Saksham Kapoor, Henry Tran, Edward Lue Chee Lip, Charlotte Le

Why I worked on it

I started this project in the summer of 2025, exploring whether a smaller model could be taught by a larger one to give feedback as effectively as a pair of larger models. The work paused for a stretch and picked back up in June 2026, and the resulting paper was accepted to the COLM 2026 ER Workshop.

What drew me in was a finding in the prior work we built on: feedback from two models tailors the response better for the student model. What we found was the opposite of what we expected. The teacher did benefit from the extra information, but the student never inherited that richness in our setup.

Methodology

The paper compared two sources of supervision for a small verifier model. In the privileged condition, the teacher could see a worked solution while scoring the steps of a candidate reasoning trace. In the non-privileged condition, the teacher scored the same traces without that reference.

Either way the 1.5B student never sees the worked solution. Both trained verifiers were then measured against a majority-vote baseline, and the two conditions had statistically indistinguishable results. Neither of them beat the baseline.

Conclusion

What I take from this paper is that a better-informed teacher does not automatically produce a better-taught student. Buying more privileged labels would not have fixed that, because the problem was the transfer, not the volume.

The follow-up sharpens the point: a 3B verifier learns substantially better from format-matched gold supervision, while the generated teacher labels stay weak at both student sizes. Scaling the student is not the fix. The question is what has to change about the feedback so its information survives the handoff.
